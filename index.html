A Personal Note

From Someone Who Has Been There

This space wasn't built out of abstract theory. It was created out of deep survival, lived experience, and a promise to pass back a lantern to anyone still in the dark.

“I survived, and I don't want you to go down that same path.”

I have been in that dark place multiple times. I know what it feels like when the weight in your chest becomes so heavy that ending everything feels like the only exit left. I have survived past suicide attempts, battled severe self-harm urges, and sat through endless hours of therapy trying to make sense of the overwhelming noise inside my head.

For a long time, I believed I was beyond repair. But bit by bit, through support, therapy, and learning to stay through one more night at a time, I found out that the darkness eventually lifts.

If you are hurting yourself or thinking about ending your life right now, please hear me: You deserve to stay. You deserve to heal. The pain you feel is real, but it is not your permanent story. Please stay. Let someone help carry the weight with you today.

Practical Coping

Riding Out Self-Harm & Crisis Urges

Urges feel like ocean waves—they build, peak, and eventually pass. You don't have to fight the wave; you just have to ride it safely.

15

Delay for 15 Minutes

Tell yourself: "I don't have to act right now. I will wait just 15 minutes." Most intense urge spikes decrease in intensity after 15 to 20 minutes.

❄️

Change Temperature

Hold an ice cube tightly in your hand, splash freezing cold water on your face, or take a cool shower. Shocking your temperature safely grounds your body.

✍️

Scribble or Release

Press a red marker hard on paper, snap a rubber band against your wrist, or tear up a heavy piece of cardboard. Release the physical tension safely.

🤝

Reach Out Out Loud

Send a simple text to a trusted friend or helpline: "I'm having a hard time staying safe right now. Can you stay on the line with me?"
